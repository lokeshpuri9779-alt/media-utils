## Local generation and review order

1. Generate frame 1 first and choose it as the character reference. Do not use a different reference for later scenes.
2. Generate frames 2–7 one at a time, reusing the same reference and settings. Do not batch blindly: inspect each frame before moving on.
3. Check the bell and rope are recognizable and consistently positioned relative to the buoy. In scene 4, the rope must visibly trap the clapper; in scene 5 the underwater loop must be legible; in scene 6 the loop must be freed; in scene 7 the rope must hang loose.
4. Reject a frame if the otter's identity changes, a required prop is missing, the action cannot be understood in one glance, or the camera hides the causal mechanism.
5. Save the seven selected stills locally as `scene-01-hook.png` through `scene-07-payoff.png`. Upload them manually into the review page's matching scene cards; the page keeps previews local and exports only filenames and your verdicts.
6. Do not interpolate or render video until all seven frames have been reviewed. This is a storyboard gate, not production approval.

## Suggested consistency settings

- Portrait 9:16; keep the otter large enough to read on a phone.
- Same checkpoint, VAE, sampler, scheduler, steps, CFG and resolution for all seven images.
- Fixed character-reference strength across the sequence; avoid changing seed/settings mid-sequence unless correcting a rejected frame.
- Keep the scene-specific camera and action intact; vary composition, not character identity or story facts.
- If your local workflow cannot use a reference image, generate a clean character reference sheet first and manually compare every frame. Text repetition alone is not visual consistency.
